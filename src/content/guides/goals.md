# Goals

Goals say where the company, a department or one person means to be by the end of a month, a quarter, a year, or any dates you choose. MannaBooks measures each one as the period goes and tells you whether it is **on track**, **at risk** or **behind**. Open them from **Work > Goals**, or see the ones running now on the **Overview**.

## Setting a goal

**New goal**, then:

1. **Name it** and, if it helps, say why it matters.
2. **Whose goal:** the whole company, a department, or a person. It can **help reach** a bigger goal, for example a Sales department goal under the company's revenue goal.
3. **Period:** a month, a quarter or a year (pick any day in it; quarters and years follow your financial year), or **Custom dates** for a push of any length. With custom dates, choose the first and last day, or the first day and how long it lasts: **1 week**, **2 weeks**, **30 days**, **6 weeks**, **90 days**, or any number of days or weeks.
4. **How it is measured**, and the **target**.

Who can set what:

- **Company goals:** people with *Goals: manage* (owners and admins have it).
- **Department goals:** the department's head, or *Goals: manage*.
- **Personal goals:** anyone, for themselves. Their department head can set and change them too.

## How goals are measured

| Measure | Where the figure comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue | Sales before VAT, from the books |
| Net profit | The profit and loss for the period |
| Costs | Cost of sales and expenses. Usually set as a limit (**at most**) |
| Cash collected | Customer payments and cash sales |
| New customers | Customers added in the period |
| Deals won | Deals marked won in the period |
| Invoices issued | Invoices issued in the period |
| Tasks done on time | The share of finished tasks done by their deadline |
| Milestones | A checklist: reached when every step is ticked |
| A number you update | Anything else. Check in with the new figure |

Money goals can be narrowed to one **project** or **cost centre**. A department's money goal counts that department's documents (and its cost centres'). Personal goals can't be measured from the books; use deals won, invoices issued, tasks on time, milestones or a number.

People without access to reports see how far along a money goal is (as a percentage) but not the figures.

## On track, at risk or behind

Each bar shows how far along the goal is, and a line shows how much of the period has gone. A goal is:

- **On track** when it is at least 95% of where it should be by now;
- **At risk** from 75%;
- **Behind** below that.

For a limit, such as costs, it is **behind** once it goes over and **at risk** when spending runs ahead of the time gone. Tasks done on time is judged against its target directly.

When the period ends, a goal shows as **Achieved** or **Missed**. A goal that no longer matters can be **dropped**, with a reason; it can be opened again.

## Check-ins and milestones

**Check in** on a goal to say how it is going. For a number you update, give the new figure there. For milestones, tick each step as it is done; whoever looks after the goal can add, change or remove steps.

The goals that help reach a bigger goal are listed on it, so you can see the company goal and every department and personal goal under it.
