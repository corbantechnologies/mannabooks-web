# Customers

A **customer** is anyone you sell to on credit or want on a receipt by name. Each one gets a number (CUS-0001) and a running balance.

## Adding a customer

1. Open **Sales > Customers** and choose **New customer**.
2. Enter their name, phone and email. Add their **KRA PIN** if they are a business: KRA needs it on the invoice for them to claim the VAT.
3. Set **payment terms** in days (for example 30). New invoices for them get a due date that many days after the invoice date.

## Balance and statement

The **balance** is what they owe: invoices less payments and credit notes. A negative balance means they have paid ahead and are in credit.

Open a customer to see their **statement**: every invoice, payment and credit note in date order with a running balance. Print it and send it when you chase payment.

A customer with documents can't be deleted. Mark them **inactive** instead to hide them from lists.

## Adding many at once

**Add many** on the Customers page gives a grid: one customer per row (name, phone, email, KRA PIN, days to pay), typed or pasted from Excel. They are saved together. A name or KRA PIN already on your list, or used twice in the grid, is stopped and shown on its row, so nobody is added twice. **Import** brings in a spreadsheet instead.

## Their own terms and conditions

If you agreed different terms with a customer (for example 60 days with no late fee), write them in **Their own terms and conditions** on their record. Their invoices, quotations, cash sales and delivery notes then carry those instead of your standard terms.

## Terms and conditions on documents

Write your standard terms once under **Settings > Terms and conditions**, one set per kind of document: invoices, quotations, cash sales, delivery notes, purchase orders (LPO) and service orders. Every new document gets a copy, printed at the bottom of the PDF, the print view and the customer's pay-online page. On an invoice the due date is added above them in words: "Payment due by 28 Nov 2026, 30 days from the invoice date." You can change the terms on a draft; once issued, the document keeps the terms it was made with, even if you change your standard terms later.
