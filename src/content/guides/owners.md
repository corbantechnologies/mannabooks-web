# Owners and directors

Money that passes between the business and the people who own or run it isn't a sale, income or an expense. When a director deposits money to keep the business going, when the business pays them back, or when an owner takes money out, record it under **Cash and banking > Owners and directors**.

Each person has their own account, like a customer statement. It shows four balances that are kept apart:

- **Capital and shares put in**
- **Owed to them**: money they lent the business
- **Owed by them**: money the business lent them
- **Drawings**

## Adding the people

Choose **Add a person**, then enter their name and whether they are a director, shareholder, owner or partner. The phone, email, ID number and KRA PIN are optional, but auditors and KRA often ask for them on director loans. Someone who leaves can be marked as no longer involved; their history stays.

**If they use MannaBooks**, choose their login under **Their MannaBooks login**. Their name and contacts fill in, and:

- they see their own balances and recent entries on their **Me** page, read-only;
- they can't record or void money on their own account, so finance does it. This keeps anyone from recording their own loan or repayment. The business owner is the exception.

The owner record is kept separate from the login on purpose. A director's loan is still owed if their login is removed, and a director who is also on the payroll keeps their salary and their loans apart. Each login can be linked to one owner record.

## Recording money

Choose **Record money**, then choose the person and what happened:

| What happened | Money | In the books |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital put in** | In | Owner's capital, for a sole trader or partnership |
| **Shares issued** | In | Share capital, and Share premium for the part paid above the shares' face value |
| **Lent to the business** | In | Directors' loan accounts, which the business owes back |
| **Loan repaid to them** | Out | Reduces what the business owes them |
| **Lent to them by the business** | Out | Loans to directors, which they owe the business |
| **Loan repaid by them** | In | Reduces what they owe the business |
| **Drawings** | Out | Drawings, an owner taking money for themselves |

Choose the bank, M-Pesa or cash account the money went through, then the amount and date. Each record posts its entry straight away, numbered **OM-**.

- **Is a director's deposit capital or a loan?** If the business is meant to pay it back, it is a loan. If it buys new shares, it is shares issued. Ask the directors, or your accountant, if you're not sure: it changes the balance sheet.
- **A repayment can't be more than the balance.** MannaBooks stops you, so a typo doesn't make a director owe money. If it really is more, tick **I've checked: record it anyway**.
- **For a loan**, you can note the date it is to be repaid. MannaBooks doesn't charge interest on director loans.

## From the bank statement

In the bank reconciliation, open a statement line that isn't in the books yet. Under **Or: money from an owner or director** (or **to**, for money out), choose the person and what it was, then **Record and match**. The record is made from the line, and the line is matched in one step.

## Balances from before MannaBooks

If a director had already lent the business money before you started, record a **Balance brought forward** for them, choosing which balance it is. It posts nothing. The total for everyone belongs in your **opening balances** (for example on Directors' loan accounts), and the brought-forward records split it by person.

## Mistakes

Void a record from the person's page. Its entry is reversed, and their balances change.

## The accounts it uses

Added to your chart of accounts the first time they are needed:

- 3000 Owner's capital
- 3010 Share capital
- 3020 Share premium
- 2550 Directors' loan accounts
- 1480 Loans to directors
- 3300 Drawings

The balance sheet shows the totals. Each person's page shows their share, and **Print** gives you their statement.

Recording needs the **Owners and directors** permission, which accountants and admins have.
