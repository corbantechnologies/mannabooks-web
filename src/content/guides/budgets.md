# Budgets

A budget is your plan for the year's income and spending, month by month. Once approved, MannaBooks compares it with the books as the year goes on.

## Making a budget

1. Open **Accounting > Budgets** and choose **New budget**.
2. Choose the **financial year**, then what it is for:
    - **A department:** covers everything tagged with the department or any of its cost centres, the same figures as *Profit and loss by department*. Its head sees it on **My department**.
    - **A cost centre** or **a project:** just that part.
    - **None of them:** the whole business.

    A cost centre without a budget of its own is checked against its department's budget when someone spends.
3. In the plan, **Add an account** for each income and expense line you want to plan.
4. Type the amount for each month, or type a **year total** in the *Year* column to spread it evenly.
5. **Save budget**.

Every budget is planned **month by month**, so each month's figure is the monthly budget, and *Budget vs actual* compares each month as well as the year to date. Weekly budgets aren't offered: a month is the shortest period most books close on.

**Copy last year** fills the plan with what each account actually came to in the same month last year, plus a percentage you choose (for example 5% for inflation). Change any figure afterwards.

## Approving

When the plan is ready, choose **Approve** (or **Send for approval** if an approval rule covers budgets). Only one approved budget can cover a given year and scope. To change an approved budget, **Reopen** it.

## Budget vs actual

The **Budget vs actual** tab shows, for each account, the budget and the actual figure from the start of the year to the month you choose, and the difference. Green means better than planned (more income, or less spending); red means worse. The bar shows how much of the year's budget has been used.

Spending is counted from the books: bills, expenses, payroll and journals posted to that account (and, for a cost centre or project budget, tagged with it).
