# Cash and banking

**Cash and banking** shows every account money sits in: bank accounts, M-Pesa tills and paybills, cash on hand and petty cash, with their balance in the books.

## Accounts

Your starting accounts (Cash on hand, Petty cash, Bank account, M-Pesa) are there already. Choose **Add account** for each other bank account or till; a matching account is added to your chart of accounts.

## Everyday money in and out

- **Customer payments** and **cash sales** (Sales) and **supplier payments** (Purchasing) already move money; you don't enter them again here.
- **Spend money** (Expenses): costs paid on the spot, such as fuel, airtime, rent or bank charges.
- **Receive money** (Other income): interest, rent you receive, commission, refunds.
- **Transfers**: money moved between your own accounts, such as banking the day's cash, sweeping M-Pesa to the bank or topping up petty cash. Enter any bank or M-Pesa charge and it goes to *Bank and M-Pesa charges*.

## Money in and out

**Cash and banking > Money in and out** shows the state of your money for the year or month chosen at the top:

- what was in your accounts at the start and at the end, and what came in and went out;
- each bank, M-Pesa and cash account with its own start, in, out and end;
- **where it came from** and **where it went** (customers, sales, owners' loans, suppliers, salaries, expenses...), from the other side of each entry, so nothing is missed however it was recorded;
- what customers still owe you and what you still owe suppliers at the end of the period;
- every movement, which you can narrow to one account or to money in or out. Open one to see its entry.

Transfers between your own accounts show on each account but are left out of the totals, since they don't change how much you have. The page needs the right to see banking, and it isn't available to someone whose access is limited to a department.

## Keeping the books right

Once a month, **reconcile** each account against its statement (see *Bank reconciliation*).

## Low-balance warnings

On an account's page, **Set one** next to *No low-balance warning* and type the lowest balance you're comfortable with. When the balance falls below it, the account shows **Below minimum** in red on Cash and banking and on your Overview, and everyone who can see banking gets one email. Another comes only after the balance has recovered and fallen again.

## Cash projection

**Cash and banking > Cash projection** shows where cash is heading if nothing new is decided, month by month for 3 to 18 months. It starts from cash and bank today, then adds and takes off everything MannaBooks knows is coming. **What's included** lists each source with how it's worked out and its total; untick one to leave it out, and click it to see what makes it up.

Money in:

- **Customers paying invoices**, in the month each is due (overdue ones in the first month);
- **Recurring invoices** still to be raised, on their schedule, after the customer's payment terms;
- **Client portfolio bills** still to be invoiced, on each service's bill date and cycle;
- **Cash sales and other income**: the average of the last three months;
- **Planned money in** that you add yourself.

Money out:

- **Supplier bills**, in the month each is due;
- **Approved purchase orders** not yet billed, after the supplier's payment terms;
- **Payroll**: the latest month's net pay and statutory deductions, every month;
- **VAT**: what's owed for past months now, then the last three months' average, each paid the month after;
- **Client portfolio supplier renewals** and **Registers** renewals (software, licences, policies), on each date and cycle;
- **Your MannaBooks subscription**, when the next payment is due;
- **Spending paid directly** (expenses): the average of the last three months;
- **Planned payments** that you add yourself.

Click a month to see what makes up its money in and out.

**Planned payments and money in** are for what isn't in the books yet: year-end bonuses, a loan repayment, a subscription, a grant. Add one with **Planned payment**: once, or every week, month, 3 or 6 months or year, with an optional end date. Something already in the books (a bill, a recurring invoice) needn't be planned again.

Set **Warn me below** (or leave it to your accounts' minimums) and it tells you the first month cash would go below it, with the chart turning red past that line. It knows only what is in the books: a guide, not a promise.

**Analytics** also shows the last 12 months as a chart: income and costs as bars, cash at each month's end as a line.
