# Work reports and tasks

Managers and supervisors can ask the people below them for a report (weekly sales and visits, a monthly site report, a daily cash summary), or give them a task to do (collect a document, fix the gate, bank the cash by 3pm), once or on a schedule. These are work reports, separate from the financial reports.

## Asking for a report

1. Open **People > Work reports** and choose **Ask for a report**.
2. Give it a title and say **what to include**. You can attach a **template** (a Word or Excel form) for people to fill in.
3. Choose **who**: everyone who reports to you (set by *Reports to* on employee records, at any depth), a department you head, or particular people.
4. Choose **how often** (once, every working day, every week, every month) and the **first due date**, and how many days before to remind them.

You can ask only people below you in the reporting line. HR managers and owners can ask anyone.

**Setting one up for someone else.** Anyone who manages work reports (HR managers, admins and owners) sees **Who it's for** at the top. Choose the person, such as the CEO, and the request becomes theirs:

- *Everyone who reports to them* means their reporting line, not yours.
- Reports, overdue notices and reviews go to them.
- The request shows them as the one who asked, and you as the one who set it up.

This lets an admin set up the reporting for managers who don't use the system much.

## What happens next

- Everyone asked gets an email, and the report appears under **Asked of me**.
- They get a reminder before it is due.
- When the due date passes (or the due time, if you set one), reports not in are marked **Overdue** and you get an email. For recurring requests, the next round opens.
- When a report comes in, you get an email. Open it under **To review**, then **Accept** it or **Send back** with what needs to change.

**Close** a request to stop further rounds.

## Giving a task

Choose **Give a task** (or **A task to do** on the request form). It works like a report request, with three differences:

- **A due time.** Set **By (time)** for something due by, say, 15:00. MannaBooks checks every hour, so it is marked **Overdue** within the hour after that time. Leave it blank for the end of the day.
- **A priority.** High-priority tasks are flagged in the list and in the email.
- **Doing it.** The person can choose **I've started**, which shows **In progress** to you, then **Mark done**, with a note or a file if they want. No write-up is needed. You then **Accept** it (it shows as **Done**) or **Send back** with what is still needed.

Tasks count in the same on-time record as reports, and show on each person's profile. A task that was started but not done by its time still counts as late.

Tasks can repeat too, for example **Bank the till cash** every week.

## The on-time record

**Work reports > On time** shows each person's record over every report due so far: how many came in **on time**, how many came in **late**, how many **never came in**, and the share on time. The lowest are at the top, so the people to follow up are easy to spot. It's useful at appraisal time.

- Only reports already due count. One still being written, before its due date, doesn't count yet.
- A report you **send back** keeps the mark of when it first came in: if it was on time, fixing it after the due date doesn't make it late.
- You see the record for the reports you asked for, or that were set up for you. Staff see their own. Anyone with full work reports access sees everyone's.

## Submitting your report

Open **Work reports > My reports**, choose the report, and write it, attach a file (PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint or a photo, up to 10 MB), or both. Then **Submit report**. It works on a phone too. If it is sent back, the reason is shown at the top; change it and submit again.
